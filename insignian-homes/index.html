From SaaS subscriptions to software it owns: how Insignian Homes is building its own CRM

Insignian Homes is one of the largest home textile exporters from India to Europe, supplying complete bedding solutions to retailers and brands in more than thirty countries. It is using Think4Ever to design and build in-house versions of its SaaS applications, starting with CRM, to cut subscription costs, reduce SaaS sprawl and own software that fits how it works with its customers.

The challenge

Insignian Homes began when a cotton farmer's grandson set out to reconnect with his family's textile heritage. Like most growing exporters, it runs on a stack of off-the-shelf SaaS applications, and its CRM is one of the largest and most important of them. Over time the costs and compromises added up:

  • Rising subscription costs. Per-seat pricing grows with every new salesperson, agent and module, and the CRM was one of the largest lines in the software budget confirm or remove.
  • SaaS sprawl. Each team added its own tools. Customer and sales data ended up spread across several applications, with overlapping features, separate logins and integrations to maintain.
  • A generic product for a specific business. Selling bedding to retailers in more than thirty countries means long B2B relationships: enquiries, sampling, quotations, seasonal ranges, repeat orders and shipments. A generic CRM forced the team to adapt its process to the software confirm the main gaps.
  • Building in-house looked too risky. Rebuilding an application like a CRM normally takes a large team and months of design work, and AI coding tools alone produce code without the requirements, data model and architecture a business-critical system needs.

The solution: design first, then build

Insignian Homes used Think4Ever to design its CRM comprehensively before building it. The team captured how the business actually works once, as objectives and business rules, and let Think4Ever generate the artifacts of the full software development lifecycle from that shared context.

StepWhat Think4Ever producedHow it helped
1. RequirementsObjectives and business rulesThe customer process, from buyer enquiry and sampling through quotation, order and shipment, is captured as objectives, business rules and acceptance criteria, then approved as intent.
2. DesignArchitecture, flows and UIThink4Ever generates the architecture, business and user flows, and UI designs, so the team can review and refine the CRM before any code is written.
3. DataDatabase schemaThink4Ever designs the data objects behind the CRM, such as buyer accounts, contacts, enquiries, samples, quotations and orders confirm objects.
4. CodeWorking applicationCoding agents build the CRM from the approved design and business rules rather than from isolated prompts.
5. Test and deployTested releaseThe CRM is tested and deployed, and the Blueprint stays current as the business changes.

A CRM has many roles, workflows, integrations and years of data behind it. Because requirements, architecture and data model are generated and approved before coding starts, Insignian Homes can match the capability of an off-the-shelf product where it matters, and leave out the modules and seats it never used.

Value across the organization

Sales and merchandising

A CRM built around how they work with buyers, with business rules they approved, instead of workarounds in a generic tool.

IT and engineering

Architecture, database schema and working code built from the approved design, plus a clear view of how a change affects the rest of the system.

Finance and leadership

Fewer subscriptions to renew and manage, predictable costs, and software the company owns as an asset.

A repeatable path out of SaaS sprawl

CRM is the first application. Because the approach is the same each time, Insignian Homes can apply it to other tools in its stack confirm candidates, e.g. sampling, order tracking, supplier management, buyer portal, consolidating overlapping tools into applications that share one data model.

Why this matters for enterprises

  • Lower SaaS costs. Replacing per-seat subscriptions with owned applications turns a growing recurring cost into a one-time build and a lower ongoing cost.
  • Less SaaS sprawl. Overlapping tools can be consolidated into applications that share one data model, with fewer logins, licenses and integrations.
  • Complex applications, designed properly. Requirements, flows, data model and architecture are reviewed before development starts.
  • Built to fit the business. The application follows the company's own process and business rules.
  • No lock-in. Everything built in Think4Ever can be exported, so the company owns its applications and their design.
